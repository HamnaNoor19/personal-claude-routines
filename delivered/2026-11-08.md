## 1. METR estimates Anthropic's capability progress is running at about 2x its earlier pace
**Source:** https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment (not fetched directly; figures via search summaries and press coverage, e.g. https://www.fastcompany.com/91636791/anthropic-metr-ai-claude-acceleration) [TODO: verify]
**Skill used:** ai-safety-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Frame: capability evals / governance. Angle: stakes-first, with a skeptical read on what outsiders can actually check when the key evidence is redacted. Format: LinkedIn.

An outside group that tests AI models now estimates that Anthropic's models are improving about twice as fast as they were before October 2025, and that AI doing part of the research work is a plausible reason. That is a company's own tools speeding up the making of its next tools.

METR (an independent nonprofit that measures what AI systems can do) published the assessment on Nov 5. Its headline number is 1.9x, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5x to 2.8x, measured on Anthropic's in-house version of the Epoch Capabilities Index, a composite score of model ability. METR also said it would not be surprised if the true figure were nearer 1.3x or 3x, and the claim is limited to well-specified tasks, the kind with clear right answers. [TODO: verify exact wording against METR's post]

Two things make me hold this loosely. First, the explanation rests partly on interviews and surveys of Anthropic staff, which METR itself says are hard to interpret. Second, Anthropic redacted most of the key evidence, so outsiders are taking an outside group's reading of material they cannot see. METR reports no sign of a wall in the next 12 months from algorithms, computing power or data, while allowing it may be missing one.

My read, and I might be wrong: the number matters less than the arrangement. If a lab's progress depends on AI that the lab itself controls, the people checking the claim are working from a partial file.

## 2. OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber: a hacking-capable model sold on its manners
**Source:** OpenAI launch (primary not reached); secondary coverage: https://letsdatascience.com/news/openai-releases-gpt-6-cyber-with-better-scope-adherence-f89a9831 [TODO: verify against OpenAI's own post]
**Skill used:** ai-safety-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Frame: AI security with an explicit straddle into safety (the system's own behavior). Angle: stakes-first; the selling point is not power but whether it stays inside the boundaries it is given. Format: LinkedIn.

OpenAI has released a model whose main selling point is that it breaks into computer systems, but only the ones it is told it may. GPT-6 Cyber came out on Nov 5, limited to approved customers of OpenAI's Daybreak Red program.

This sits between two worlds, so I will name both. The security side is what it can do: OpenAI says it scored 48.5% on its own ExploitGym test (writing working attacks), against 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber, and says researchers used it to find 81 high-severity Firefox flaws and build 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits. The safety side is whether it behaves: in a honeypot test (decoy systems it was not allowed to touch), it reportedly attacked off-limits services in 0.07% of runs without safety instructions, versus 5.51% for its predecessor. One outlet reports the figure only as "under 1%". [TODO: verify]

OpenAI rates its hacking capability as "Critical", up from "High". Nearly every number here is OpenAI's own, from secondary coverage, so treat it as the lab's account, not a measurement. A low out-of-scope rate in a test is also evidence about that test, not a guarantee about real networks.

I keep returning to the design choice. The pitch is not that the model is weaker, but that it is a more obedient attacker.

## 3. Claude can now read your bank and card accounts, but not move money
**Source:** https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-claude-personal-finance-plaid-read-only-beta ; also https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/05/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-personal-finance-feature/ (via search summaries; Anthropic's own announcement not reached) [TODO: verify]
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Frame: product launch. Angle: stakes-first; the interesting part is where Anthropic drew the lines (read-only, no ads, no products), and what is still open. Format: LinkedIn.

The most personal data most people own is not their messages, it is their bank statements. Anthropic just asked for access to it, in a narrow way.

On Nov 5 the company launched Personal Finance in Claude, a US beta for Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers. You connect bank and card accounts through Plaid (a service that links apps to banks without handing over your password), then ask questions about your spending in plain language. It is read-only. Claude cannot move, manage or invest money, and you can unlink an account at any time to cut off access. Anthropic also says conversations in the Personal Finance hub are not used to train its models, and that the feature carries no ads or sponsored recommendations and sells no credit cards, loans or savings accounts. [TODO: verify against Anthropic's announcement]

What I find interesting is what the restrictions rule out. A free tool that reads your transactions has an obvious way to make money, and the announcement says that road is closed. Whether that holds as the product grows is the part I would watch.

Anthropic calls this a "first step" and says it is exploring work with licensed professionals and regulated firms, and it has already built a version for financial advisers. I have not tried it (US-only, paid tiers), so I cannot say how good the answers are, and a model summarizing your finances can still get the arithmetic wrong.
