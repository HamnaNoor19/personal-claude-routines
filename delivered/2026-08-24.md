## 1. An AI designed real drug-discovery molecules, and outside labs checked its work

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/Claude-accelerates-protein-design
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Capability evaluation / hands-on demo — stakes-first, built around the human-vs-model comparison on one target as the concrete, memorable receipt.

---

Anthropic just published something I had to read twice: an AI model designed real drug-discovery molecules, and outside labs confirmed they actually work.

The task was building protein binders — molecules engineered to stick tightly to one specific target in the body, usually the first hard step in making a new drug. Anthropic gave Claude Opus 4.8 and an unreleased model called Mythos Preview 15 biological targets and let them generate 1,320 candidate designs on their own, choosing binding sites and running structure-prediction tools without a scientist steering each step. Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience then tested the designs in a real wet lab, not a simulation.

354 of those designs bound to their targets. That's 14 out of 15 targets covered, with hit rates between 22.6% and 35.1% depending on setup, against a typical industry rate of 10% to 15%. On one target, a regulatory protein called RBX1, Mythos Preview hit 40%. Human scientists who tackled that same target in an earlier Adaptyv Bio competition managed 3.7%.

I want to be careful about what this does and doesn't show. Anthropic supplied a roughly 16,000-word protocol and up to 12,500 Nvidia H100 GPU hours, and the model leaned on existing open-source tools (RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, ESMFold2) rather than inventing new methods. This is one campaign, on binding, which is an early step, not a finished drug. It's not evidence that AI replaces the wet lab or the years of work after a binder is found.

What it does show is that a model can run a real scientific workflow end to end, at a hit rate above what human-designed pipelines typically achieve, and have the result checked by an independent lab rather than take Anthropic's word for it. That combination is the part worth sitting with.

## 2. Nvidia sells the shovels. Now it's reportedly buying a stake in the mine.

**Source:** https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-30b-perplexity-bet-extends-093340421.html
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Industry news / business — synthesis with this week's Nvidia–Poolside deal, reading the pattern rather than either deal in isolation.

---

Nvidia sells the chips almost everyone trains AI on. This week it's reportedly lining up to own a slice of a product built on top of them too, at a valuation north of $30 billion.

The Information reported Nvidia is in talks to invest in Perplexity, the AI search company, at a valuation above $30 billion. That's more than 50% higher than the roughly $20 billion Perplexity was valued at in its last round about a year ago. The number behind that jump: Perplexity's annualized revenue has gone from under $250 million at the start of the year to more than $750 million now, with a chunk of that growth coming from Perplexity Computer, an agent product that automates tasks on a user's computer rather than just answering search queries.

Neither company has confirmed the talks, so treat the valuation as a reported figure, not a done deal.

The timing is what makes this worth tracking more than the valuation number. Days earlier, Nvidia structured a $6 billion deal with the coding startup Poolside that its own investor letter explicitly called "not an acquisition." Now there's a reported nine-figure-plus check heading toward a search company too. Individually, either deal reads as a normal strategic investment. Together, they look like a pattern: a chip company that used to just sell picks and shovels is now taking stakes in the companies mining with them, at the model layer and the product layer, without the acquisitions that would draw antitrust scrutiny.

I don't think that makes Nvidia's compute business less dominant. I think it makes it harder to say where the compute business ends and the model and product layer begins.

## 3. Alibaba launched a flashy AI video model. The balance sheet next to it is the real story.

**Source:** https://dataconomy.com/2026/08/24/alibaba-launches-wan30-a-30-second-ai-video-generation-model/
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Industry news / product launch — frame-shift: most coverage is treating this as a video-model launch; the sharper story is the capex-vs-profit bet sitting right next to it.

---

Alibaba launched a new AI video model this week. The more interesting number is on the page right next to it: the company's quarterly profit just fell 75%.

The model is Wan3.0, which can turn a document, spreadsheet, or slide deck into up to 30 seconds of video, and it's already been used in short dramas, ads, and marketing since its public beta opened on August 6. Alibaba made the official launch one day after closing a $10 billion share sale, reportedly the largest follow-on stock offering ever by a Hong Kong-listed company, with the money earmarked for Qwen model infrastructure and cloud compute.

The part that got buried under the product news: Alibaba's most recent quarterly net profit dropped 75%, to 10.44 billion yuan, while capital spending jumped in the other direction, up 75% to 67.68 billion yuan. AI cloud revenue did grow, up 45% to 48.44 billion yuan, and Alibaba has reportedly floated raising its three-year AI infrastructure commitment from 380 billion yuan to 480 billion yuan.

Look at what Alibaba is actually asking shareholders to accept this week: profit fell by three-quarters, it just raised $10 billion, and it's reportedly considering raising its AI spending target even further. Wan3.0 is the proof of work it's pointing to while it makes that ask.

Every major AI company is running some version of this trade right now: near-term profit against long-term infrastructure bets. Alibaba's version is just unusually visible, because the losses and the launch landed in the same news cycle.
