## 1. Anthropic's Economics team: AI helps with the writing and analysis of science, not the lab bench
**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-researchers-use-claude
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Research report / stakes-first: the speedup AI can give science is capped by how much of a field is physical work, and the report puts numbers on that. Format: LinkedIn.

Scientists are using Claude for the parts of research that happen on a laptop. The parts that happen at a lab bench are barely touched, and that gap may decide how much AI speeds up science.

Anthropic's Economics team published a study of 207,049 scientific sessions on Claude's consumer plans (Aug 2 to Sep 30, 2026). A language model labeled each session by field and task, and Anthropic says only the labels were kept. Computer science was about 4.3% of researchers but 11.9% of Claude chats and 32.7% of Claude Code sessions, the coding-focused tool. By task, Claude skews to writing (32.0% of sessions versus 16.0% of researcher time) and away from gathering data (11.5% versus 28.6%). Ideation, the work of coming up with hypotheses, is 4.2% versus 7.1%.

The part I keep thinking about is the scenario model. The authors assume every step that doesn't need hands or equipment gets automated. Fields with little physical work, like statistics and computer science, then speed up roughly 15 to 16 times. Biology, chemistry and materials science reach only 3 to 4 times, because physical steps are about 38 to 41% of researcher time there, versus 4% in the median field. That is a stylized model with stated assumptions, and the authors call the scenarios illustrative.

Caveats worth carrying with the numbers: the field labels come from an automated classifier, the data covers consumer plans only, and the survey of 528 scientists (about 40% expect AI to run full research projects by 2030) came from people who applied for Anthropic's AI for Science credits, so they are probably heavier AI users than most. This is also Anthropic studying its own product.

My read, and it is speculative: if this holds up, "AI will speed up science" is really several different stories, and the slow one is the one about medicine and materials. Robots and lab automation might matter more there than smarter models.
