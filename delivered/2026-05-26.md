## 1. Pope Leo XIV's "Magnifica Humanitas" — the AI encyclical with an Anthropic co-founder at the Vatican

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/news/chris-olah-pope-leo-encyclical  
**Skill used:** ai-safety-post-crafter  
**Frame / angle:** Governance / policy — frame-shift: the news is written as "Pope weighs in on AI"; the actual story is what the Anthropic co-founder said while standing there.

---

The most striking thing about *Magnifica Humanitas* — the papal encyclical on AI released yesterday — isn't what the Pope said. It's who was in the room.

Chris Olah, one of Anthropic's co-founders and the researcher who largely built the field of mechanistic interpretability (the science of understanding what's happening *inside* AI models, not just what they output), stood at the Vatican alongside cardinals and theologians to respond publicly. He is, by his own description, an atheist.

His remarks were unusually direct: every frontier AI company, including Anthropic, operates inside incentive structures that can conflict with doing the right thing. He called on religious institutions, civil society, and governments to push back — specifically as "moral voices that the incentives cannot bend." He asked for "informed critics."

Pope Leo's text is sweeping. He frames AI explicitly as the labor question of this century, deliberately echoing *Rerum novarum*, the 1891 document on industrial-era workers' rights. Specific concerns: AI concentrating power in a handful of wealthy nations, AI deployed in warfare, AI-generated misinformation targeting children, and labor exploitation in the rare-earth supply chains that power the hardware.

Taken at face value, this marks a major moral institution entering a governance debate where technical expertise has so far crowded out other voices. What's more speculative is whether any concrete mechanism follows. The Washington Post framed it as Anthropic "aligning with the Vatican over the White House" on AI oversight — a framing that, if accurate, says something about where one frontier lab has decided to plant its flag.

I don't know what to make of an atheist interpretability researcher asking the Pope to help hold his company accountable. I don't think it's cynical. I think it might be the most honest public statement about AI self-governance I've read from inside the industry.

## 2. Anthropic's $30B round and first-ever profit projection

**Source:** https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-22/anthropic-to-close-over-30-billion-round-as-soon-as-next-week  
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter  
**Frame / angle:** Industry news — stakes-first: the revenue reversal is the real story, not the valuation headline.

---

Last summer, Anthropic told investors it didn't expect a profitable year until at least 2028. This quarter, it's projecting $559 million in operating profit.

That context matters more than the valuation headline. Anthropic is reportedly closing a $30 billion+ funding round this week at a $900 billion+ valuation — which would make it the most valuable AI startup in the world, passing OpenAI's $852 billion March figure. But the more interesting number is the revenue trajectory.

Annualized revenue was around $9 billion at the end of last year. By April 2026, it had crossed $30 billion. Q2 revenue is projected at $10.9 billion, up 130% from $4.8 billion the quarter before. That pace — tripling an annualized run-rate in roughly six months — is rare even by the standards of fast-scaling software companies. It's closer to what you'd see in a utility that just found its infrastructure moment.

The round itself does more than signal market position. Rounds of this size, with Sequoia, Dragoneer, Altimeter, and Greenoaks each reportedly committing around $2 billion, tend to flow into compute: training infrastructure, inference capacity, the hardware bets that show up in model quality 12 to 18 months later. How much of this capital goes toward new model training versus deployment scaling will shape what Anthropic ships in 2027.

A few caveats worth keeping: Bloomberg's reporting says the deal isn't signed yet — these are talks, not a closed agreement. The revenue figures are investor disclosures, not audited results. The shape of the story holds; the exact numbers are still in motion.

## 3. Post-trained LLMs recognize their own voice (arxiv:2605.25459)

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25459  
**Skill used:** ai-safety-post-crafter  
**Frame / angle:** Interpretability — personal-noticing: fine-tuning creates a measurable self-recognition signal, and this paper found the specific internal mechanism.

---

Fine-tuning an AI model into an assistant changes more than its outputs — it creates an internal distinction between "my voice" and "foreign text." A new paper from Anthropic and IAS Princeton found the signal that drives this.

The paper by Asvin G. and Jack Lindsey (submitted to arxiv yesterday) measured this across model families and sizes: when fine-tuned models generate their own typical responses, their probability distribution over the next word is 3-4x more concentrated than when they're processing text they wouldn't normally produce. They're measurably more "committed" to their own outputs.

Tracing the mechanism: the model maintains an internal representation of how "surprising" the most recent input was relative to what it would have predicted. When that surprise signal is low (the model is generating normally), outputs narrow. When it's high (encountering text it wouldn't have written), outputs diffuse. The authors demonstrate this causally, not just as a correlation.

Worth being deliberate about what this doesn't mean: nothing here implies the model is self-aware, has goals, or has anything like an inner life. The authors say this plainly. What it does show is that fine-tuning creates internal structure — traceable at the level of individual signals, not just behavior — between acting and processing.

My read on the safety angle: if you can instrument this signal, you'd have a potential probe for detecting when a model is being driven off its normal behavior. Prompt injection and jailbreaks often work exactly this way, making the model process and continue text it wouldn't have generated itself. The paper doesn't make that leap. I am. Worth watching the follow-up.
