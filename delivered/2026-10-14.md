## 1. Anthropic says it has cut what model-copiers can harvest by 90 to 99%, and names two labs
**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/distillation-defenses (primary, Oct 12, 2026, read directly)
**Skill used:** ai-safety-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Frame: AI security (model extraction). Angle: stakes-first, with a skeptical read of a self-reported number. Frames not taken: governance/export-control, alignment.

If you sell access to an AI model, anyone with a credit card can try to copy it by asking it a few million questions and training a rival on the answers. Anthropic published its defenses against that on Oct 12.

The practice is called distillation (training a new model on an existing model's answers). Anthropic says the part it most wants to block is extraction of Claude's step-by-step reasoning, which is far more useful for training than final answers. Its reported result: the amount of reasoning text a copier can collect fell by about 90 to 99% in early data. Two measurements back that up. A model trained on summarized reasoning scored 71.8% on SWE-bench Verified (a coding test) versus 80.4% when trained on full reasoning. And after a new two-stage filter went live, extractable reasoning dropped 10x to 100x or more, with a stated false-positive rate under 0.1%.

The post also names names. It says Zhipu distilled cyber capabilities while building GLM-5.3, and that requests to Moonshot's Kimi models were secretly forwarded to Claude. Tens of thousands of linked accounts have been banned, often within two hours of a campaign starting. These are Anthropic's allegations in its own post; I haven't seen a response from either company. ## 2. Anthropic's election red-team found Claude refuses one bad request but can miss a campaign split into harmless-looking pieces
**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-us-midterms (primary, Oct 14, 2026, read directly)
**Skill used:** ai-safety-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Frame: AI security / evals. Angle: frame-shift. Coverage will likely lead with the 99% evenhandedness score; the more useful finding is the red-team's multi-step gap. Frames not taken: governance, political-bias debate.

Ask an AI chatbot to write a fake news story and it will often say no. Ask it for twenty small, innocent-looking pieces that add up to a fake news site, and, according to Anthropic's own election red-team, it can miss that.

That is one finding in Anthropic's Oct 14 midterms update. Outside testers at the Institute for Strategic Dialogue and the McCain Institute probed Claude in the US, Israel and Brazil. Claude reliably refused single harmful requests but could miss malicious campaigns split into individually harmless tasks. Anthropic says it is adding classifiers (automated detectors) aimed at multi-step misuse, such as fake social accounts and fake news sites. It also describes real cases: operators running a fake news site used Claude to write US midterm coverage favoring Russian, Iranian and North Korean narratives, and others generated fabricated content for ad revenue. Those accounts were banned.

Some scale from a sample of 250,000 US Claude.ai conversations (Sept 21 to 27): about 0.51% were about elections or voting, roughly 0.01% asked who to vote for, and about 0.002% violated election-integrity policy. Anthropic expects these shares to rise nearer Election Day.

The headline number is 99% on its political evenhandedness evaluation for the Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 models. That test is Anthropic's own, so I'd weigh the three outside reviews more; one of them found an older model, Sonnet 4.5, softened right-leaning arguments more than left-leaning ones. Claude also sometimes added unsolicited caveats.

What this shows: where one lab's safeguards had a gap, and what it says it's patching. What it doesn't show: whether the fix works, since there's no after-fix number yet.

## 3. Anthropic's government MOU in Singapore has real usage numbers behind it but no dates or targets in it
**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/news/singapore-office-opening (primary, Oct 14, 2026, read directly)
**Skill used:** ai-post-crafter
**Frame / angle:** Format: LinkedIn post. Frame: industry news / business. Angle: skeptical read, separating the numbers (adoption) from the agreement (plans only). Frames not taken: model release, safety.

Singapore ranks second of 121 countries in Claude.ai usage relative to population, according to Anthropic. That's the number I'd start with in today's announcement of its Singapore office and government agreement.

The agreement, a memorandum of understanding (a non-binding statement of intent) with the Ministry of Digital Development and Information, was signed by co-founder Ben Mann at Singapore International Cyber Week. It lists cooperation on model evaluation and testing standards, incident response, AI skills for public officers, help for small businesses, and sharing data from the Anthropic Economic Index. The post gives no dates and no numeric targets for any of it.

The adoption side has more detail. Over 1,500 GovTech Singapore engineers use Claude Code. Grab's sales assistant is credited with about 40,000 hours saved per quarter.
